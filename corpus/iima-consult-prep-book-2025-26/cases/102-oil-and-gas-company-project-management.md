# Oil and Gas Company Project Management

## Case at a glance

- **Case type:** Unconventional
- **Source pages:** 279-281
- **Candidate sees initially:** the opening brief only.
- **Facilitator sees:** the ordered source turns and approach material below.

## Opening brief

Your client is a major Oil & Gas player. They were going to expand their refining capacity by Dec 2023. However, due to policy changes and competitive pressure, they now want to do it by Dec 2022.

## Facilitator interview script

> Speaker labels are reconstructed from the source slide’s shaded interviewer callouts. `Source sequence` marks a turn that needs human speaker review before runtime approval.

<!-- Source PDF page 279, column 1 -->

### Turn 1 - Candidate

Can you tell me a little more about the client – which part(s) of the Oil & Gas industry does it operate in? – Only downstream or midstream and upstream as well? What is the geographical footprint of the company?

### Turn 2 - Interviewer

It is an Indian public sector player operating primarily in the downstream segment.

### Turn 3 - Candidate

I see. What is the current refining capacity and refinery wise-split of the company? What is the targeted capacity?

### Turn 4 - Interviewer

Their current capacity is 15 MMTPA of crude oil processed split between two refineries – 10 MMTPA and 5 MMTPA. They want to expand their 5 MMTPA plan to 12 MMTPA.

### Turn 5 - Candidate

Interesting. So, am I correct to assume that our objective is to help the client shorten the expansion project completion time from Dec 2023 to Dec 2022, i.e., shorten it by a year?

### Turn 6 - Interviewer

Yes, that’s correct.

### Turn 7 - Candidate

Okay. I would like to propose an overall strategy here. First, I want to understand the current status and map out the proposed phase-wise timeline. Then we can proceed to analyzing the time proposed for each step and the sequencing to identify possibilities for crashing the processes. Finally, we can look at the risks and additional finances, if any, associated with the schedule and possible measure to prevent and mitigate the challenges in case of a delay.

### Turn 8 - Interviewer

That seems reasonable. Go ahead.

### Turn 9 - Candidate

Great. I would like to first look at all the inputs and setup requirements for the expansion plan. Mainly I would want to focus on capital requirements, licenses and permissions, raw material inputs like crude oil supply and land acquisition. Next, I would want to look at the design and planning phase. Herein, I would want to focus on the design process and timelines, tendering process (as applicable), After that, we can look at the equipment procurement, construction and installation phase. Finally, ways to expedite the testing and inspection phase can be looked at. Did I get the stages correctly or is there anything that I missed?

### Turn 10 - Interviewer

Yes, you got them.

### Turn 11 - Candidate

Could you share the expected timeline for each phase? Also, is there any particular bucket you want me to analyse first?

### Turn 12 - Interviewer

Sure. So, let's assume we have the capital requirements, fuel supply, licenses and land acquisition figured out. The design and tendering processes are expected to take around 6 months. The ensuing procurement and installation of equipment is expected to take 3 years. Finally, the inspection and testing should last some 6 months, setting us up for operations 4 years from now.

### Turn 13 - Candidate

Got it. So, we need to crash the process by around 12 months reducing it to 36 months

### Turn 14 - Interviewer

Correct.

<!-- Source PDF page 279, column 2 -->

### Turn 15 - Candidate

Okay. I would like to start with looking at the design and planning phase. I have a few questions regarding the same: Is the design of the plan done in-house or through external consulting? • • What is the current status of the design plan? • Is it done phase wise or is the complete design done at once before moving on to the next stage?

### Turn 16 - Interviewer

Currently we have identified an outside EPC for the designs. They have quoted a total time of 6 months for all the deliverables.

### Turn 17 - Candidate

I see. Is there any particular reason why we are not doing it phase wise? We can get the designs in stages and start commissioning their fabrication. To optimize this, we can ask the EPC to start with the design that requires maximum time for fabrication, delivery and installation.

### Turn 18 - Interviewer

That seems a good approach. So, we know that the main reactor takes the maximum time, which is, 3 years, for fabrication and delivery. No other equipment takes more than 2 years for the same.

### Turn 19 - Candidate

Interesting. Can we ask the EPC to prioritize the design of the reactor? If yes, by when can we expect them to deliver the same? Additionally, what would be the cost implications of the same?

### Turn 20 - Candidate

The EPC can deliver the reactor design in 1 month. Do not worry about the costs.

### Turn 21 - Candidate

Great. That should crash our project time by 5 months as all other equipment will anyhow take less than 2 years post the commissioning. Is that correct?

### Turn 22 - Candidate

Yes, that can be done. Let’s move on to the next phase.

### Turn 23 - Candidate

I would like to understand the fabrication and delivery process better. My current understanding is that once the designs from the EPC are received, a tender would be floated for the fabrication of the equipment. Once the contractor is selected, the fabrication would begin. The final step would be the delivery and installation of the equipment.

### Turn 24 - Candidate

That’s correct. We expect the tendering process to take up to 3 months. The fabrication is expected to take another 2 and a half years for the reactor. The delivery and installation should take another 3 months.

### Turn 25 - Candidate

I would like to take them up one by one. I am also assuming that all other equipment have a much shorter lead time of under 2 years and the reactor is the bottleneck that we need to investigate.

### Turn 26 - Candidate

That’s true.

### Turn 27 - Candidate

First looking at ways of expediting the tendering process, 3 months seems like a long time. Is there any particular reason for the same?

### Turn 28 - Candidate

Since these are highly specialized equipment and the potential contractors are often not apprised of the requirements before hand, we like to keep a 2 month bidding window to give them time to prepare and bid for the same.

<!-- Source PDF page 280, column 1 -->

### Turn 29 - Source sequence

I see. Can we reduce this window? This might result in some additional costs as the bids might not be as competitive as before. We can mitigate the same by ensuring that our tender is very detailed. Also, doing it only for the reactor first should make it easier for the bidders.

### Turn 30 - Source sequence

We can reduce it to the minimum window of 2 weeks.

### Turn 31 - Source sequence

What about the fabrication? What is the mode of transport used for the delivery?

### Turn 32 - Source sequence

The fabrication is a black box for us. Not much we can do about it. The delivery depends on the contractor used. 3 months for international contractors and 15 days for an Indian one.

### Turn 33 - Source sequence

I am guessing selectively going for an Indian vendor might result in some additional costs. Are there any other concerns?

### Turn 34 - Source sequence

No, that’s about it. We can look into the same.

### Turn 35 - Source sequence

Great. Additionally, we can incentivize the contractor for early completion of the work.

### Turn 36 - Source sequence

Good. Let’s move on to the inspection phase.

### Turn 37 - Source sequence

Can you help me understand the inspection and testing process.

### Turn 38 - Source sequence

Sure. Inspection and testing is done in cycles of 15 days. Usually there are a lot of failures due to specification mismatches and compatibility issues with the current plant. So various iterations are needed. Certification is done by DGH officials who very often take months to give an appointment for an inspection visit. The subsequent paperwork is also bureaucratic and can take weeks.

### Turn 39 - Source sequence

I see. Can we take some precautionary steps to ensure that failures are minimized? Our engineering team can work closely with the EPC and the equipment manufacturer to minimize such instances. We can leverage our connections with the government to expedite the DGH approval process.

### Turn 40 - Source sequence

Great. Let’s end the case.
