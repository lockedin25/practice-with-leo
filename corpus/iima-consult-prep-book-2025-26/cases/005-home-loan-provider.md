## Opening brief

Your client is an affordable home loan provider. They started in 2018-2019 and have grown to have a book size of 1500 crore by 2022. However, the book size has remained unchanged for the last 8/9 months. What do you think are the reasons for this, and what can we do to help them?

## Facilitator interview script

> Speaker labels are reconstructed from the source slide’s shaded interviewer callouts. `Source sequence` marks a turn that needs human speaker review before runtime approval.

<!-- Source PDF page 47, column 1 -->

### Turn 1 - Candidate

Great. So, a couple of questions before we dive into the solution. What do we mean by affordable home loans?

### Turn 2 - Interviewer

They provide loans with small ticket sizes, usually between 10 to 15 lakhs

### Turn 3 - Candidate

Is this stagnation an industry-wide trend given the current economic conditions? Additionally, what does our competition look like? How many direct competitors are there, & what’s their market share?

### Turn 4 - Interviewer

It’s not an industry-wide issue. It’s something that has happened to our client only. We have around 4 competitors in the market, and all 5 players have an equal market share.

### Turn 5 - Candidate

Since it’s not an industry-wide issue and our book size has not increased over the last 8-9 months, is it safe to assume our 20% share has fallen and been taken up by our competitors?

### Turn 6 - Interviewer

Yes. We don’t have the latest numbers, but we expect them to indicate a significant fall in market share for us.

### Turn 7 - Candidate

Okay, understood. So, to assess the reasons for this stagnation, I would like to look at it from demand and supply sides both. On the demand side, I would like to look at the number of consumers and the average ticket size per consumer. On the supply side, I’d like to evaluate if any internal constraints are impacting our ability to disperse loans, such as regulatory authorities' sanctions on our bank.

### Turn 8 - Interviewer

We can focus on the demand side for now.

### Turn 9 - Candidate

Okay. Breaking down demand into the number of customers and average loan ticket size per customer. Have we seen a fall in any of these numbers?

### Turn 10 - Interviewer

The average ticket size has remained the same.

### Turn 11 - Candidate

So, this implies that our number of customers has fallen. Have we seen an increase in repayments, or should I focus on just the fresh loans we are getting?

### Turn 12 - Interviewer

Good question. We can ignore repayments for this case.

### Turn 13 - Candidate

Got it. So, to understand why the number of customers could have gone down, I would like to work through key elements of their journey – identification of need, competition analysis, selection of provider, processing of their loan, disbursement of loan and post-disbursement activities. We already know that demand hasn’t gone down since the competitors aren’t facing this problem, so I’d like to focus on the other elements. Is there anything specific you would like me to work through?

### Turn 14 - Interviewer

Yes, we know that the problem is in the processing stage. How would you break it down further?

### Turn 15 - Candidate

We can look at the following stages: document collection, submission, verification and approval.

### Turn 16 - Candidate

Looks good. What next?

<!-- Source PDF page 47, column 2 -->

### Turn 17 - Candidate

I’d like to walk through each of these elements and see where the customers are dropping out. What is our way of document collection & submission – do customers submit it online or do they come to the branch to submit it?

### Turn 18 - Interviewer

Customers can initiate applications online or request home pickup of documents. A dedicated team manages this entire process. After this, the verification and approval processes follow.

### Turn 19 - Candidate

Great. We can talk about document verification and approval now. Based on my understanding of the banking industry, I believe there would be a multi-level approval process that would be followed – starting from initial approvals at the local branch manager level and moving up to the regional level depending on the customer’s risk/total loan requirement. Have we seen any change in the approval process – have any new levels been added or have the rejections at this stage gone up?

### Turn 20 - Interviewer

That’s correct. We’ve recently added a centralized approval layer at the zonal office to ensure tighter control on the process. However, the number of loans getting rejected at this stage has gone up.

### Turn 21 - Candidate

Interesting. Is there any reason for this increase? Are the customer documents not meeting specific criteria or are there any deviations not identified earlier?

### Turn 22 - Interviewer

The approver is being extra cautious and asking for more customer documents. There is no fault at the previous levels of verification.

### Turn 23 - Candidate

Understood. Given how we collect the documents from our customers from their homes, is it correct to assume that we now must travel back with these documents to their house and collect the updated ones again?

### Turn 24 - Candidate

Yes. What do you think are the implications of this?

### Turn 25 - Candidate

This would cause our turnaround time to go up significantly which could lead to dropouts.

### Turn 26 - Candidate

Correct. That is what is happening. What are some recommendations you would give them?

### Turn 27 - Candidate

My recommendation would be – 1) Analyze the abnormalities being identified by the zonal approver and see if we can incorporate them in our standard documentation so we can remove the incidence of this at the source 2) Relook the need for a centralized zonal approval 3) In the long run, we can consider moving to an online/app-based set-up so the turnaround time for the entire process can be reduced significantly

### Turn 28 - Candidate

Great, I think we can end the case here.

### Turn 29 - Candidate

Thank you for your time.
