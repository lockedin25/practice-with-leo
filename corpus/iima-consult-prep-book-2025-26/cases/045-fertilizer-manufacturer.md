# Fertilizer Manufacturer

## Case at a glance

- **Case type:** Growth Strategy
- **Source pages:** 144-145
- **Candidate sees initially:** the opening brief only.

## Opening brief

Your client is a Fertilizer manufacturer supplying all commonly used fertilizers to the farmers across the country. They want to increase their revenue. Give them recommendations for the same.

## Facilitator interview script

> Speaker labels are reconstructed from the source slide’s shaded interviewer callouts. `Source sequence` marks a turn that needs human speaker review before runtime approval.

<!-- Source PDF page 144, column 1 -->

### Turn 1 - Candidate

Thank you for the problem statement. Does the client have a quantitative revenue figure in mind?

### Turn 2 - Interviewer

Yes, they want to grow at 10-12% while the industry is growing at 4-5%.

### Turn 3 - Candidate

Can I know more about the fertilizer? Is there anything unique about the fertilizer? Is it applicable for all crops or only certain crops?

### Turn 4 - Interviewer

The fertilizer is a standard NPK fertilizer that can be applied on all crops.

### Turn 5 - Candidate

Where does the firm geographically operate?

### Turn 6 - Interviewer

The firm operates in most parts of the country, barring Northeastern states where it is not as feasible for us to transport the product.

### Turn 7 - Candidate

Thanks for that information. Just to make sure I'm on the right track, should I focus only on strategies to increase revenue, or do I need to consider cost management as well?

### Turn 8 - Interviewer

You should only focus on increasing revenue for now.

### Turn 9 - Candidate

Okay, we can look at increasing the revenue organically and inorganically.

### Turn 10 - Interviewer

You can leave out inorganic growth and instead, focus on organic growth.

### Turn 11 - Candidate

To grow organically, we can either increase prices or sell more products. Since fertilizers are usually similar across brands, raising prices might not be a good idea. So, we should focus on selling more. Is this a fair assumption?

### Turn 12 - Interviewer

Yes, your assumption is correct.

### Turn 13 - Candidate

If we look at the existing markets, do we know how well our products are currently penetrating these markets? Are there areas where we could improve our distribution or marketing efforts?

### Turn 14 - Interviewer

We haven’t fully explored that, but it’s worth considering.

### Turn 15 - Candidate

We should also think about our marketing strategy. We could segment our customer base by region, crop type, or even the size of the farm, allowing us to target specific groups more effectively. For example, we could position our fertilizer as the go-to choice for high-yield crops in regions where farmers are focused on maximizing productivity.

<!-- Source PDF page 144, column 2 -->

### Turn 16 - Interviewer

Is there anything else we should consider?

### Turn 17 - Candidate

To get our fertilizers into more hands, expanding our dealer network could be effective. One option could be to enter new markets; countries like Sri Lanka and Bangladesh could be potential options. Do we know whether our customers are large dealers?

### Turn 18 - Interviewer

90% of our sales come from large dealers, while the remaining 10% come from small dealers.

### Turn 19 - Candidate

So, large dealers are key players for us. If we wanted to strengthen our relationship with them, we could offer incentives. Besides offering better incentives, another strategy could be to bypass the dealers altogether and sell directly to the farmers.

### Turn 20 - Interviewer

That’s an interesting idea. How would you suggest we go about it?

### Turn 21 - Candidate

There are a couple of approaches we could take. First, we could partner with government agricultural bodies that are already in contact with farmers. We could run educational workshops through these bodies to show farmers how our fertilizer can help increase their crop yields. Does the company have any experience working with government programs?

### Turn 22 - Interviewer

Not much, but it’s something we could explore.

### Turn 23 - Candidate

Another approach would be to collaborate with private agricultural groups. These groups often have strong leaders who influence the farmers' decisions. We could offer them special programs or incentives, like discounts on bulk purchases or exclusive access to new product lines, to encourage them to promote our fertilizers. If we pursue this strategy, we should also think about how to measure its success.

### Turn 24 - Interviewer

That sounds like a solid approach.
