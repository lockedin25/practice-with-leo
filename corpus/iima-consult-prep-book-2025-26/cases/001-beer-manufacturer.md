# Beer Manufacturer

## Opening brief

Your client is a beer manufacturing company that operates in India. The cost structure for the company is poor as compared to the international benchmarks, and you have been asked by the client to find out why.

## Facilitator interview script

> Speaker labels are reconstructed from the source slide’s shaded interviewer callouts. `Source sequence` marks a turn that needs human speaker review before runtime approval.

<!-- Source PDF page 39, column 1 -->

### Turn 1 - Candidate

Is the problem only on the cost side, or should I also analyze the revenue side to look for a problem there?

### Turn 2 - Interviewer

No, the problem is only on the cost side.

### Turn 3 - Candidate

Alright. Is the problem with only one specific beer product, or is the problem spread out across different products? Additionally, is this an industry wide problem, or specific to our client?

### Turn 4 - Interviewer

The problem is not limited to one product. In fact, the client is facing the problem across many beer products. Our key competitors, most of which are international players, haven’t really been facing this issue.

### Turn 5 - Candidate

Okay. I think it would make sense to break the costs down into the value chain to see where the cost issue lies. Should I go ahead with the same?

### Turn 6 - Interviewer

Sure, you can do that.

### Turn 7 - Candidate

I would start by breaking this into the value chain. The components that I would like to analyze are as follows: R&D, Raw Material Procurement, Processing, Storage & Transportation, Distribution and finally Marketing. Does this sound okay or should I look into something more as well?

### Turn 8 - Interviewer

The value chain seems fine.

### Turn 9 - Candidate

Next, I would like to understand which component contributes to the maximum deviation in costs so that I can analyze accordingly.

### Turn 10 - Interviewer

Good question! The processing costs seem to be the issue.

### Turn 11 - Candidate

Okay, in that case, I would like to delve deeper into processing costs. However, before I proceed with the same, I would like to see if this is the only cost head that has an issue.

### Turn 12 - Interviewer

You may proceed with processing costs. There are no other cost heads where the client is facing a problem.

### Turn 13 - Candidate

Okay. The possible sub-heads to analyze processing costs would be rent, electricity expense, machinery, technology, labour hours and wages, capacity utilization, packaging, efficiency (defects). Should I analyze all these heads or is there a particular head with an issue?

### Turn 14 - Interviewer

Yes. The client is indeed facing a problem in the utilization of capacity.

### Turn 15 - Candidate

So, capacity utilization can be broken down as: Actual Output/Potential Output. Is the utilization with the current capacity 100%?

### Turn 16 - Interviewer

Yes, current capacity utilization is 100%.

### Turn 17 - Candidate

This means we should look into potential capacity. How many plants and machines is the client currently operating?

### Turn 18 - Interviewer

As of now, the client has 3 plants with 3 machines each. Total capacity of each plant is 30,000 bottles.

### Turn 19 - Candidate

How is this figure when compared with the competitors of the client?

### Turn 20 - Interviewer

Competitors are manufacturing 50,000-80,000 bottles per plant.

<!-- Source PDF page 39, column 2 -->

### Turn 21 - Candidate

This difference might be due to more plants, more machines/plant or better efficiency of machines being used.

### Turn 22 - Interviewer

Rightly said. The difference is due to better efficiency of machines which are primarily imported from China.

### Turn 23 - Candidate

Okay. Then client might not have been able to install better machinery due to reasons either internal or external to the client. Under internal reasons, we will majorly look at the two broad reasons: 1. Monetary – gross initial cost of machinery, availability & attractiveness of financing, and total operations cost 2. Non-monetary – accessibility to the seller(s), logistical issue in procuring the machinery, lack of skilled labour force. Which of these applies to the client?

### Turn 24 - Interviewer

The cost of the new machines is 1.5 times the normal machine, which is not a challenge for the client to invest and there is no special training needed for the labour force to operate the machine. There is no accessibility problem as well. Let’s explore the external reasons.

### Turn 25 - Candidate

Alright, under the external reasons, I will look at the following factors: Economic – interest rates or exchange rates are not favorable to this transaction 1. 2. Environmental – the alternative machine might not comply with the environmental standards 3. Political – unstable relations between the 2 countries might pose as a hindrance Legal – importing such machines might require special licenses/permits or machine standards might not be 4. in line with the law of the land Social – Indian consumers have been pushing for fully made in India products which might put additional 5. constraints on Indian companies 6. Technological – tools & other components which are available might not be suitable for alternate machine

### Turn 26 - Interviewer

Great – the Indian government has put restrictions on import of several Chinese products and technology, and this machine is under that list. Thus, we have not been able to move up from the lower capacity machine. What would be your recommendations?

### Turn 27 - Candidate

Has the client looked for similar machinery? Or have they consulted the authorities regarding this issue?

### Turn 28 - Interviewer

No, we have been made aware of the new machinery quite recently.

### Turn 29 - Candidate

Alright, in that case, I will recommend 3 measures: 1. Short-term: look for similar vendors – The client can look at other countries where similar machines with a higher capacity are available. Since investment ability is not an issue for our client, then even if we pay a bit extra, we can cover that through scaling production. 2. Medium-term: lobby with authorities – Several import restrictions have been withdrawn if they have harmed the interests of domestic businesses. Client can approach the government for lobbying in this matter. 3. Long-term: Design and develop machine internally – In the long run, the client can design and develop its own technology to expand production based on its own needs. Alternatively, the client can also acquire design licenses and then develop the machine in India.

### Turn 30 - Interviewer

That’s great. We can close the case here.
