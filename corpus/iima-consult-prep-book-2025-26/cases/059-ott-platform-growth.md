# OTT Platform Growth

## Case at a glance

- **Case type:** Growth Strategy
- **Source pages:** 175-178
- **Candidate sees initially:** the opening brief only.
- **Facilitator sees:** the ordered source turns and approach material below.

## Opening brief

Your client is a pan-India video streaming OTT platform like Hotstar, Netflix etc. Due to the competitive industry their growth has stagnated. It wants you to devise a growth strategy.

## Facilitator interview script

<!-- Source PDF page 175, column 1 -->

### Turn 1 - Candidate

I would like to understand the objective of our client. What does he mean by growth? Is he referring to the top line of the company or bottom line of the company?

### Turn 2 - Interviewer

The client wants us to focus on topline growth and ignore the cost side. Okay. Does the client have any targets, timelines or budget in mind? Are they open to exploring new businesses

### Turn 3 - Candidate

or expanding outside India?

### Turn 4 - Interviewer

Look at a 2-fold strategy with plans for short-term (1 year) and long-term growth. There are no specific targets. Ignore budgetary constraints and foreign expansion. Stick to the present business.

### Turn 5 - Candidate

Alright, I will explore ways to achieve both short-term and long-term growth. Further, can I know a little about the business model of the company?

### Turn 6 - Interviewer

The company has a freemium model and has an app and website. Its free version is monetized by advertising revenue and has a total user base of 30 Mn people. The premium version is available for INR 150/ month and has a total subscriber base of 3 Mn people. Okay. So, is there a particular segment you want me to focus on for this growth strategy?

### Turn 7 - Interviewer

Yes. Please focus only on the premium customer base.

### Turn 8 - Candidate

Sure. I would like to know a little about the platform. What content do we offer and in what languages? Is there any content difference between the free and premium models? Do we produce our own content or do we license 3rd it from parties?

### Turn 9 - Interviewer

We offer entertainment content like movies, TV shows and short films across various genres. Our content is both in English and Hindi. A large chunk of our English content is produced in-house while all our Hindi content is licensed. In-house content is only available on the premium version. This holds true even with some of our licensed content.

### Turn 10 - Candidate

Great. Can I know a little about our user demographic? Also, do we have any idea on user perception of our content?

### Turn 11 - Interviewer

Our content is well received by the users. Within English we have noticed that our in-house content receives much more traction than licensed content. Most of our users are urban youth.

### Turn 12 - Candidate

Is this trend purely due to the quality of our in-house content or are there any other factors at play?

### Turn 13 - Interviewer

A part of it is due to our content quality. But there are 2 more contributing factors. Firstly, a lot of licensed content is also available on other platforms which reduces their demand. Also, we follow extensive marketing for our in-house content which results in its high popularity.

### Turn 14 - Candidate

Ok. Can I know what the competition in this market looks like?

### Turn 15 - Interviewer

80% of the market consists of a few major players (Netflix, Prime, Hotstar, Sony). Rest is fragmented.

### Turn 16 - Candidate

Okay. I have enough information to proceed. Growth can be through 2 major levers – Organic and inorganic growth. I will first focus on organic growth.

<!-- Source PDF page 175, column 2 -->

### Turn 17 - Interviewer

That looks fine. How do you plan to achieve Organic growth?

### Turn 18 - Candidate

Revenues from premium segment can be divided into number of customers and revenue per customer. However, both these are interconnected. I will focus on increasing number of customers. This can be done in 2 ways - increasing our target market size or our share in the target market.

### Turn 19 - Interviewer

How do plan to increase the target market size?

### Turn 20 - Candidate

Given that we are urban-centric, we can look at increasing our presence in tier-2 and below areas. This will require targeted marketing and might also need the introduction of regional content.

### Turn 21 - Interviewer

We don’t plan to enter the regional content market or divert focus to tier-2 cities as we feel there is still a lot of untapped potential in tier-1 urban areas.

### Turn 22 - Candidate

Alright. Then, we can look at diversifying from entertainment content by going into sports, news etc. This require a lot of content acquisition and is more of a long-term plan.

### Turn 23 - Interviewer

That seems interesting but is a massive jump from where we are. Limit your analysis to the current content base that we have. Why don’t you look at increasing the market share of the existing business?

### Turn 24 - Candidate

To increase our market share we can focus on content-related factors or non-content-related factors. In non- content related factors, we can look at improving platform factors like UI/UX, suggestion algorithms, account sharing prevention or look at widespread marketing.

### Turn 25 - Interviewer

We are already on par with major competitors when it comes to the factors you mentioned. What else can we explore?

### Turn 26 - Candidate

Then we can look at tweaking our pricing to see if overall revenues increase. Is the Rs 150/ month the only payment plan we have? How are competitors priced?

### Turn 27 - Interviewer

Yes, that is our only pricing plan. Aside from Netflix, we charge more than our competitors.

### Turn 28 - Candidate

Considering, the competitive industry we can look at reducing our prices. But a detailed analysis needs to be performed on whether the resulting market share increase will compensate the price reduction.

### Turn 29 - Interviewer

Good point. Let's say that reducing prices will lead to larger revenues. How will you implement this?

### Turn 30 - Candidate

Instead of outright reducing the prices permanently, discounted limited-time offers can be given. This will create subscription urgency and leave the door open for any future price increases. Additionally, we should add, annual plans, mobile-only plans and distinct subscription tiers to offer greater flexibility to subscribers. I feel that all this combined will help us realize our short-term growth.

### Turn 31 - Interviewer

Great. How do you plan to achieve the longer-term growth we target?

### Turn 32 - Candidate

Here we can look at content-related factors. Given that our in-house English content is performing well we should keep that up. The big gap I see is with our Hindi content. Looking at the insights from our English segment, I feel we should start producing in-house Hindi content.

<!-- Source PDF page 176, column 1 -->

### Turn 33 - Interviewer

Good point. But our in-house production team doesn’t have experience in the Hindi segment.

### Turn 34 - Candidate

We can develop this capacity from scratch but going the acquisition route for inorganic growth might be easier. Are there any Hindi content producers in the market that we can acquire?

### Turn 35 - Interviewer

There is a small company ABC which distributes original content via its YouTube page and website. It has around 5 Mn YouTube subscribers and its content is well liked. But its website, meant for paid users has low traction. They are struggling to monetize their high-quality content. Given their potential and the issues they are facing, ABC can be a viable target. I shall evaluate this acquisition

### Turn 36 - Candidate

by looking at financial and non-financial factors. Under financial factors, I shall explore the benefits and the costs. Under non-financial factors, I shall explore cultural fit and legal aspects. Starting with the benefits – is it fair to assume that a chunk of ABC’s loyal users will switch to our premium service upon acquisition? Plus, there would also be non-ABC users who would switch from our free model to our premium model.

### Turn 37 - Interviewer

Yes, that is fair. Assume that ~0.5 Mn users will be added due to the ABC brand and ~1.5 Mn of our free users will turn premium.

### Turn 38 - Candidate

Great, that would mean ~2 Mn new premium users added over a period due to the acquisition. Do we know how much we may have to pay for acquiring them?

### Turn 39 - Interviewer

Due to monetary issues faced by the target, we believe that we can acquire them for ~INR 90 Cr in a cash plus stock deal. By those numbers, I see that we are acquiring ~2 Mn premium users at a cost of INR 450 per user (i.e., 90 Cr/

### Turn 40 - Candidate

2Mn). What is our present customer acquisition cost? Can we finance the INR 90 Cr deal?

### Turn 41 - Interviewer

Yes. There are no issues in financing this deal. Our current customer acquisition cost is Rs 500 which is also around the industry average. Great. This deal seems to present us with a huge upside even without considering any future new users outside

### Turn 42 - Candidate

of our firm and ABC. This deal will also offer massive synergies – more bargaining power with production houses and advertisers; and overall better value proposition to users. Further, due to digital nature of the business and small scale of ABC, integrating it with us won’t be a hurdle.

### Turn 43 - Interviewer

Great. Why don’t you analyze the non-financial aspects of this deal? Both companies cater to youth, and I feel that giving ABC creative freedom & retaining their top management

### Turn 44 - Candidate

will preserve their existing culture and content quality. We must also be careful in the legal structuring of this deal to ensure that we get exclusive access to their past and future content.

### Turn 45 - Interviewer

Perfect. Why don’t you briefly outline the post-acquisition risks and considerations?

### Turn 46 - Candidate

While ABC has produced exceptional content in the past, there is a major risk in if they will continue to do so. Hence, giving them creative freedom and any additional support will be critical in the initial stages. What works for us is that as ABC's management will also hold company stock after this deal, they will have a major incentive to perform well. Further, we must consider if widening our Hindi content base will have any negative impact on English-friendly subscribers who may move to other niche platforms. Another critical decision we must take is under whose name to brand ABC's new content and what to do with their YouTube Channel.

### Turn 47 - Interviewer

Great. We can take those up in later discussions. Let’s close the case here. Thank you.
