# Food & Beverage (F&B) & Restaurants Industry Dossier

## 1. Industry Overview & Macro Dynamics

### 1.1 Core Business Model & Format Segmentation
The restaurant and foodservice industry converts perishable food ingredients into freshly prepared meals and dining experiences. It operates on thin net margins (5–15%), high operational intensity, and hyper-local catchment dynamics (typically a 2–5 km radius per store).

### 1.2 Macro Trends Shaping F&B
1. **The Delivery Shift:** Off-premise dining (delivery + takeaway) has surged from <15% to 35–50% of total revenue for casual dining and QSRs, transferring pricing power and customer ownership to food delivery aggregators (DoorDash, UberEats, Zomato, Swiggy).
2. **Input Cost Volatility:** Extreme swings in edible oils, dairy, poultry, vegetables, and packaging materials requiring frequent menu re-engineering or shrinkflation.
3. **Real Estate Squeeze:** High street and premium mall rental escalations forcing operators into revenue-share leases with minimum guaranteed rents (MG vs. Rev-share of 12–18%).

### 2.1 Central Kitchen (Commissary) vs. In-Store Prep Model
- **Central Commissary Advantages:**
  - Standardized recipes and consistent taste profile across 50–500 outlets.
  - Bulk purchasing discounts (5–12% lower raw material cost).
  - Shrinks in-store kitchen footprint from 40% of store area to 20%, releasing 20% more space for revenue-generating dining seats.
  - Reduces in-store skilled chef requirement to lower-cost assembly line staff.
- **Trade-offs:** High refrigerated cold-chain distribution costs, daily delivery logistics complexity, shelf-life management, and upfront Capex for the central processing plant.

## 3. Revenue Models & Monetization Mechanics

### 3.1 Dine-In Revenue Engine
$$\text{Dine-In Revenue} = \text{Total Seats} \times \text{Average Seat Occupancy (\%)} \times \text{Table Turns per Day} \times \text{Average Spend Per Head (SPH)}$$
- **Table Turns:** Number of distinct dining parties served per table during lunch and dinner services (e.g., 2.5 turns at dinner = 6:30 PM, 8:00 PM, 9:30 PM).
- **Spend Per Head (SPH) Drivers:** Food item pricing, beverage attach rate (soft drinks / high-margin alcohol), appetizer & dessert attach rates, combo upsells.

### 3.2 Delivery & Off-Premise Revenue Engine
$$\text{Delivery Revenue} = \text{Total Orders} \times \text{Average Order Value (AOV)} \times (1 - \text{Aggregator Commission \%}) + \text{Packaging Fees Charged}$$
- **Channel Friction:** Third-party delivery aggregators charge 18–25% commission on gross order value. Restaurants often deploy dual pricing (marking up menu prices 15–20% on delivery apps compared to dine-in) to preserve gross margins.

### 3.3 Menu Engineering Matrix (BCG / Kasavana-Smith Matrix)
Items are classified by **Profitability (Unit Contribution Margin)** vs. **Popularity (Sales Volume)**:

```
                  High Profitability (Contribution Margin)
                                  ^
                                  |
            [PUZZLES]             |            [STARS]
    - High Margin / Low Volume    |    - High Margin / High Volume
    - Action: Reposition, promote,|    - Action: Maintain quality,
      re-price, rename menu item  |      prominent menu placement
    ------------------------------+-------------------------------> High Popularity
            [DOGS]                |           [PLOWHORSES]            (Sales Volume)
    - Low Margin / Low Volume     |    - Low Margin / High Volume
    - Action: Eliminate from menu |    - Action: Increase price,
      or substitute ingredients   |      reduce portion, re-engineer
                                  |
```

## 6. Essential KPI Formulas & Benchmarks

### 6.1 Prime Cost Percentage
$$\text{Prime Cost (\%)} = \frac{\text{Total Food \& Beverage COGS} + \text{Total Direct Labor Cost}}{\text{Total Gross Revenue}} \times 100$$
- **Benchmark:**
  - **Healthy:** $\le 55\%$ for QSR; $\le 60\%$ for Casual Dining.
  - **Dangerous / Insolvent:** $>65\%$ (business will generate negative net cash flow after rent and utilities).

### 6.2 Food Cost Percentage & Theoretical vs. Actual Variance
$$\text{Actual Food Cost (\%)} = \frac{\text{Beginning Inventory} + \text{Purchases} - \text{Ending Inventory}}{\text{Food Sales Revenue}} \times 100$$
$$\text{Theoretical Food Cost (\%)} = \frac{\sum (\text{Menu Item Quantity Sold} \times \text{Standard Recipe Cost})}{\text{Food Sales Revenue}} \times 100$$
$$\text{Variance (Waste \& Theft \%)} = \text{Actual Food Cost (\%)} - \text{Theoretical Food Cost (\%)}$$
- **Benchmark:** Variance should be $<1.5\%$. A variance $>3\%$ signals severe kitchen waste, improper portioning, supplier short-weight deliveries, or employee pilferage.

### 6.3 Table Turnover Rate (Table Turns)
$$\text{Table Turns per Meal Period} = \frac{\text{Total Parties / Covers Served}}{\text{Total Available Tables / Seats}}$$
- **Dwell Time Interlock:**
  $$\text{Maximum Theoretical Turns} = \frac{\text{Operating Window (e.g., 4 hrs)}}{\text{Average Dining Dwell Time (e.g., 1 hr)} + \text{Table Busing/Reset Time (10 mins)}}$$
- **Benchmark:**
  - QSR: 6–10 turns/day (Dwell time: 15–25 mins)
  - Casual Dining: 2.5–4.5 turns/day (Dwell time: 45–65 mins)
  - Fine Dining: 1.0–1.8 turns/day (Dwell time: 90–150 mins)

### 6.4 Revenue Per Available Seat Hour (RevPASH)
$$\text{RevPASH} = \frac{\text{Total Hourly Sales}}{\text{Total Available Seats} \times \text{Operating Hours}} = \text{Seat Occupancy (\%)} \times \text{Average Spend Per Head}$$
- Used by restaurant revenue managers to measure hourly utilization across day-parts (Breakfast, Lunch, Late Afternoon, Dinner, Late Night).

### 6.5 Same-Store Sales Growth (SSSG) / Like-for-Like (LFL) Sales
$$\text{SSSG (\%)} = \frac{\text{Sales from Stores Open }>12\text{ Months (Current Period)} - \text{Sales from Same Stores (Prior Period)}}{\text{Sales from Same Stores (Prior Period)}} \times 100$$
- Filters out expansionary revenue growth from opening new outlets; isolates organic brand health and price vs. footfall growth.

### 7.1 Case Scenario 1: Casual Dining Chain Margin Collapse
**Problem Statement:** A 40-unit casual dining Italian restaurant chain has seen store-level EBITDA decline from 18% to 6% despite a 15% increase in total top-line sales. Diagnose the cause.

**Diagnostic Protocol:**
1. **Sales Channel Mix Disaggregation:**
   - Total Sales grew 15%, but **Dine-in revenue fell by 20%**, while **Delivery revenue surged by 120%** (Delivery grew from 15% to 50% of total revenue).
2. **Channel Contribution Comparison:**
   - *Dine-in ticket:* \$32.00 per head $\times$ 68% Gross Margin - 20% Labor - 12% Rent = **18% Store EBITDA**.
   - *Delivery ticket:* \$20.00 ticket $\times$ 62% Gross Margin - 22% Aggregator Commission - 5% Packaging - 8% In-App Ad Boost - 15% Labor = **3% Store EBITDA**.
   - *Root Cause:* Channel cannibalization. High-margin dine-in customers shifted to delivery; the delivery channel carries 22% platform commissions + 8% ad spend + packaging costs, collapsing overall store margins.
3. **Turnaround Recommendations:**
   - Implement **Channel-Specific Dynamic Pricing:** Mark up delivery menu prices by 15–18% relative to dine-in to absorb aggregator commissions.
   - Launch **Direct Ordering Channel:** White-label app/WhatsApp ordering with 5% loyalty cashback, converting delivery customers to 0% commission direct delivery.
   - Re-engineer Dine-In Experience: Introduce weekday prix-fixe lunch menus, live acoustic music, and happy-hour beverage promotions to drive dine-in footfall and high-margin alcohol sales.

## 8. Common Pitfalls & Candidate "Landmines"

1. **Assuming High Delivery Volume Automatically Increases Profit:**
   - *Landmine:* Suggesting a casual dining restaurant solve its profitability problems by partnering aggressively with food delivery apps.
   - *Reality:* Third-party delivery aggregators charge 20–25% commission and induce heavy discounting. Without menu price markups or dedicated low-cost kitchen lines, delivery sales can severely cannibalize high-margin dine-in footfall.

2. **Neglecting Prime Cost as the Unified Operating Anchor:**
   - *Landmine:* Analyzing food cost and labor cost in isolation.
   - *Reality:* Food cost and labor are interdependent. Pre-cut, pre-marinated vegetables from a commissary increase food COGS by 3% but reduce in-store labor by 6%, resulting in a net 300 bps improvement in Prime Cost. Always evaluate **COGS + Labor** together.

3. **Confusing Top-Line Sales Growth with Same-Store Sales Growth (SSSG):**
   - *Landmine:* Praising a restaurant chain for growing system revenue 30% when all growth came from opening 40 new stores while existing store SSSG fell by 8%.
   - *Reality:* Negative SSSG with unit expansion indicates brand exhaustion, market saturation, or customer cannibalization.

4. **Ignoring Table Dwell Time Dynamics during Peak Hours:**
   - *Landmine:* Recommending complex dessert or coffee upselling during Friday night peak dinner rush.
   - *Reality:* Upselling a \$5 dessert that prolongs a 4-person table's dwell time by 30 minutes prevents seating a new party that would generate a \$120 check. In peak hours, **table turn velocity** generates vastly more profit than minor incremental item upselling.
