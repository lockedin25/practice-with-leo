# Banking & Financial Services (BFSI) Industry Dossier

---

## 1. Industry Overview & Macro Dynamics

Commercial banking operates on a fundamental financial intermediation engine: mobilizing liabilities (deposits and borrowings) at an interest cost, and deploying those funds into assets (loans, advances, and treasury securities) to earn a higher yield, while managing credit, liquidity, interest rate, and capital risks.

```
                    ┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
                    │               COMMERCIAL BANKING ENGINE                 │
                    └────────────────────────────┬────────────────────────────┘
                                                 │
         ┌───────────────────────────────────────┴───────────────────────────────────────┐
         ▼                                                                               ▼
┌─────────────────────────────────────────┐                     ┌─────────────────────────────────────────┐
│     LIABILITIES & FUND MOBILIZATION     │                     │     ASSET DEPLOYMENT & ADVANCES         │
│ • Low-Cost CASA (Current/Savings)       │      INTERMEDIATION │ • Retail Loans (Mortgage, Auto, Unsec.) │
│ • Term / Fixed Deposits (Retail & Corp) │ ──────────────────► │ • SME & Commercial Business Banking     │
│ • Wholesale / Interbank Borrowings      │       SPREAD (NIM)  │ • Large Corporate Syndicate Facilities  │
│ • Subordinated Tier II Debt             │                     │ • Treasury & Sovereign G-Sec Portfolio  │
└─────────────────────────────────────────┘                     └─────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

### Global vs. Indian Regulatory & Operating Landscape

| Dimension | Global / Developed Markets (US, EU) | India & Emerging Markets (RBI Framework) |
|---|---|---|
| **Deposit Base Structure** | Lower CASA reliance; high securitization / wholesale debt funding | Highly deposit-driven; CASA accounts for **35% – 45%** of total deposits for top private banks |
| **Statutory Reserve Mandates** | Minimal explicit reserve requirements (focus on LCR / NSFR) | **CRR (Cash Reserve Ratio)**: ~4.5% locked at 0% interest with RBI<br>**SLR (Statutory Liquidity Ratio)**: ~18% mandated in low-yield Government Securities |
| **Priority Sector Mandates** | Community Reinvestment Act (US) | **Priority Sector Lending (PSL)**: Mandates **40% of Adjusted Net Bank Credit (ANBC)** allocated to agriculture, MSMEs, affordable housing, and weaker sections |
| **NIM Benchmark** | 2.5% – 3.2% (compressed in zero/low rate environments) | **3.5% – 4.5%** (higher structural interest rate spreads) |
| **Capital Adequacy (Basel III)** | Minimum CET1 7.0%, Total CAR ~10.5% | Stricter RBI standards: Minimum Total CAR **11.5%** (inclusive of 2.5% Capital Conservation Buffer) |

### Value Chain Decomposition

| Stage | Operating Activities | Cost & Operational Metrics | Margin & Economic Drivers |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Deposit Mobilization** | Branch network, corporate payroll mandates, wealth management cross-sell | Cost of Deposits (CoD), CASA ratio %, blended Cost of Funds (CoF) | High CASA mix lowers blended cost of capital, expanding Net Interest Margin (NIM) |
| **2. Customer Sourcing & Underwriting** | Direct Sales Agents (DSA), branch RM sourcing, credit scoring (CIBIL/Experian), collateral valuation | Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC), Turnaround Time (TAT), Approval % | Algorithmic underwriting lowers processing costs; risk-based loan pricing |
| **3. Loan Disbursement & Servicing** | Escrow release, core banking booking, interest rate resets, repayment schedule management | Processing fee realization (0.5%–1.5%), loan servicing cost | Upfront fee income offsets loan origination operational costs |
| **4. Asset Monitoring & Surveillance** | Daily DPD (Days Past Due) tracking, Early Warning Signals (EWS), SMA-0, SMA-1, SMA-2 classification | Slippage ratio %, Gross NPA additions | Proactive credit intervention prevents SMA-2 accounts from slipping into Non-Performing Assets (NPAs) |
| **5. Collections, Restructuring & Recovery** | Call-center dunning, field recovery agencies, SARFAESI collateral seizure, NCLT/IBC insolvency | Recovery rate %, Credit Cost %, Legal & recovery agency fees | Maximizing cash recoveries and minimizing provisioning write-offs |

## 3. Revenue Models & Monetization Mechanics

Commercial banks monetize through two primary engines: **Fund-Based Net Interest Income (NII)** and **Non-Fund Fee & Other Income**:

```
                               ┌──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
                               │                 BANKING REVENUE STREAMS                  │
                               └────────────────────────────┬─────────────────────────────┘
                                                            │
                 ┌──────────────────────────────────────────┴──────────────────────────┐
                 ▼                                                                     ▼
    ┌───────────────────────────────┐                                     ┌───────────────────────────────┐
    │  FUND-BASED INCOME (75-85%)   │                                     │  NON-FUND FEE INCOME (15-25%) │
    │ • Interest on Retail Loans    │                                     │ • Loan Processing & Late Fees │
    │ • Interest on SME / Corp Loans│                                     │ • Letters of Credit / Guarantees
    │ • Yield on Treasury Securities│                                     │ • Forex & Hedging Margins     │
    │ • Interbank Lending Yields    │                                     │ • Wealth / Insurance Distrib. │
    └───────────────────────────────┘                                     └───────────────────────────────┘
```

### 1. The Net Interest Spread Mechanics
$$\text{Net Interest Spread (NIS)} = \text{Yield on Advances (YoA)} - \text{Cost of Funds (CoF)}$$
$$\text{Net Interest Income (NII)} = \text{Total Interest Income Earned} - \text{Total Interest Expenses Paid}$$

### 2. Loan Portfolio Yield Profiles by Asset Class

| Loan Asset Category | Typical Yield on Advances (YoA) | Credit Loss Risk Profile | Capital Consumption (RWA %) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Prime Home Loans (Mortgages)** | **8.25% – 9.25%** | Ultra-low defaults (<0.5%); fully collateralized | Low (35% – 50% risk weight) |
| **Auto & Vehicle Loans** | **9.5% – 11.5%** | Moderate default risk (1.0%–2.0%); movable collateral | Moderate (75% risk weight) |
| **SME & Commercial Banking** | **10.5% – 13.5%** | Working capital churn; sensitive to economic cycles | High (100% risk weight) |
| **Unsecured Personal Loans & Credit Cards** | **14.0% – 28.0%** | High default risk (3.5%–7.0%); zero collateral | Very High (125% – 150% risk weight) |
| **Large Corporate Loans (AAA)** | **7.5% – 8.75%** | Thin margin; consortium structured; high ticket | Moderate (20% – 50% depending on rating) |

|---|---|---:|

### Key Operational Leverage Drivers
1. **The CASA Multiplier**: Current Accounts pay 0% interest; Savings Accounts pay 3.0%–3.5%. If a bank increases its CASA ratio from 30% to 45%, its overall Cost of Funds drops by 60–90 bps, translating directly into hundreds of millions in pre-tax profits.
2. **Digital Channel Migration**: Branch transaction cost (~$2.00–$3.00) vs Mobile/UPI transaction cost (<$0.05). Transitioning routine services to mobile apps drives Cost-to-Income below 40%.
3. **Credit Risk Asymmetry**: Lending is an asymmetric risk game. A bank earns 3% net spread on a good loan, but loses 100% of principal on a total default. Rigorous underwriting protects against catastrophic tail risk.

## 6. Essential KPI Formulas & Benchmark Ranges

### Profitability & Spread Metrics

| Metric | Formula | Normal Benchmark Range |
|---|---|---|
| **Net Interest Margin (NIM)** | $$\text{NIM} = \frac{\text{Net Interest Income (NII)}}{\text{Total Average Interest-Earning Assets}} \times 100$$ | High-performing Private Banks: **3.8% – 4.5%**<br>PSU Banks: **2.6% – 3.2%** |
| **CASA Ratio (%)** | $$\text{CASA Ratio} = \frac{\text{Current Account Deposits + Savings Account Deposits}}{\text{Total Deposits}} \times 100$$ | Industry Leading: **40% – 48%**<br>Average: **30% – 35%** |
| **Cost of Funds (CoF)** | $$\text{CoF} = \frac{\text{Total Interest Expense on Deposits and Borrowings}}{\text{Average Total Interest-Bearing Liabilities}} \times 100$$ | Best-in-class: **4.2% – 5.2%** |
| **Cost-to-Income Ratio (CIR)** | $$\text{CIR} = \frac{\text{Total Operating Expenses (Employee + Opex)}}{\text{Net Interest Income + Non-Interest Income}} \times 100$$ | **Top Quartile: 38% – 45%**<br>(>55% indicates branch bloat) |

### Asset Quality & Capital Adequacy Metrics

| Metric | Formula | Normal Benchmark Range |
|---|---|---|
| **Gross NPA Ratio (GNPA %)** | $$\text{GNPA \%} = \frac{\text{Total Gross Non-Performing Advances}}{\text{Total Gross Advances}} \times 100$$ | Clean Asset Quality: **<2.0%**<br>Stressed Portfolio: **>5.0%** |
| **Net NPA Ratio (NNPA %)** | $$\text{NNPA \%} = \frac{\text{Gross NPA} - \text{Total Provisions Made}}{\text{Gross Advances} - \text{Total Provisions Made}} \times 100$$ | Best-in-Class: **<0.5%** |
| **Provision Coverage Ratio (PCR)** | $$\text{PCR \%} = \frac{\text{Total Cumulative Provisions Held against NPAs}}{\text{Total Gross NPAs}} \times 100$$ | **Target: 70% – 85%** |
| **Credit Cost Ratio** | $$\text{Credit Cost} = \frac{\text{Net Provisioning Expenses for Loans in Year}}{\text{Average Total Advances}} \times 100$$ | Normal: **0.5% – 1.0%**<br>Crisis: **>2.5%** |
| **Capital Adequacy Ratio (CAR / CRAR)** | $$\text{CAR \%} = \frac{\text{Tier I Capital (CET1 + AT1)} + \text{Tier II Capital}}{\text{Total Risk-Weighted Assets (RWA)}} \times 100$$ | Regulatory Minimum: **11.5%**<br>Comfortable Cushion: **15.0% – 18.5%** |

#### Diagnostic Execution Protocol:
1. **Deconstruct NIM**:
   - Check if CASA % fell because depositors moved funds to higher-yielding fixed deposits or equity mutual funds.
   - Check if the bank lowered lending rates on corporate loans to gain market share without lowering deposit costs.
2. **Audit Credit Quality (Slippage & Provisions)**:
   - Check if GNPA jumped due to slippages in unsecured personal loans or mid-market SME segments.
   - High provisioning directly reduces Pre-Provision Operating Profit (PPOP) to a fraction at the Net Profit level.
3. **Examine Branch Productivity**:
   - Calculate CASA mobilized per branch and per employee across vintage cohorts (branches >3 years old vs new branches).

## 8. Common Pitfalls & Candidate "Landmines"

1. **Confusing Loan Disbursements with Top-Line Revenue**:
   - *Candidate Error*: Treating $10B in new loan disbursements as $10B of bank revenue.
   - *MBB Reality*: Loan disbursements represent Balance Sheet asset expansion. Revenue is only the **interest earned** (e.g., $10B × 9% = $900M) plus upfront processing fees (1% = $100M).

2. **The "Just Raise Fixed Deposit Rates" Blunder**:
   - *Candidate Error*: Recommending that a bank solve its liquidity squeeze by offering an aggressive 8.5% interest rate on fixed deposits.
   - *MBB Reality*: A sharp spike in term deposit rates leads existing low-cost savings account (CASA) customers to switch funds to fixed deposits. Blended Cost of Funds skyrockets, destroying Net Interest Margins.

3. **Overlooking Credit Provisions and Non-Performing Assets (NPAs)**:
   - *Candidate Error*: Diagnosing a bank's profitability decline purely as an OpEx/salary issue without examining the loan loss provision line.
   - *MBB Reality*: In banking, credit cost volatility easily overwhelms OpEx. A 200 bps increase in NPA slippages can wipe out an entire year's pre-tax profit regardless of branch cost-cutting.

4. **Ignoring Capital Adequacy (CAR) Constraints on Growth**:
   - *Candidate Error*: Recommending the bank aggressively grow its loan book by 40% annually to boost interest income.
   - *MBB Reality*: Loan growth consumes Risk-Weighted Assets (RWA). If the bank's Tier 1 Capital Adequacy ratio falls below statutory limits (11.5%), the regulator will impose Prompt Corrective Action (PCA), restricting further lending and dividend payouts.

5. **Treating All Deposits as Identical**:
   - *Candidate Error*: Treating bulk corporate wholesale deposits the same as sticky retail CASA deposits.
   - *MBB Reality*: Corporate wholesale deposits are volatile, rate-sensitive, and prone to rapid withdrawal during market stress, whereas retail CASA deposits are granular, sticky, and provide the bedrock of bank franchise valuation.
