# Fast-Moving Consumer Goods (FMCG) & CPG Industry Dossier

---

## 1. Industry Overview & Macro Dynamics

The Fast-Moving Consumer Goods (FMCG) / Consumer Packaged Goods (CPG) sector encompasses high-velocity, non-durable consumer items across Food & Beverages, Personal Care, Home Care, and Over-the-Counter (OTC) wellness. The industry is defined by massive consumer reach, brand-equity-driven pricing power, extensive multi-tier physical distribution networks, and fierce shelf-space competition.

### Global vs. Indian / Emerging Markets Context

| Dimension | Developed Markets (US, Western Europe) | India & Developing Markets |
|---|---|---|
| **Channel Structure** | Dominated by **Modern Trade** (Walmart, Tesco, Carrefour) accounting for 70%–85% of sales | Dominated by **General Trade (Kiranas)** (12M+ unorganized mom-and-pop stores) driving **70%–80%** of volume; Quick Commerce & E-commerce surging to 10%–15% in metros |
| **Price Point Architecture** | Multi-pack / family-size formats; high absolute price points ($5–$25) | **Price-Point Constrained (INR 5, INR 10, INR 20 LUP sachets)**; price points remain fixed while grammage is dynamically engineered ("shrinkflation") |
| **Working Capital Model** | Neutral to slightly positive working capital | **Negative Working Capital Cycle** for blue-chip leaders (HUL, Nestlé, ITC) driven by cash distributor advances and 60–90 day supplier credit |
| **Private Label Threat** | High (retailer private labels capture 20%–35% volume share) | Low but growing in Modern Trade (7%–10%); branded trust and emotional loyalty dominate General Trade |

- **The Bullwhip Trap**: When a brand offers aggressive quarter-end primary discounts, distributors overbuy. Primary sales spike while secondary offtake is flat, causing distributor warehouses to choke with unsellable inventory, triggering stock returns and order halts in the subsequent quarter.

### The Working Capital Cycle Engine (Negative Working Capital)

$$\text{Cash Conversion Cycle (CCC)} = \text{Days Inventory Outstanding (DIO)} + \text{Days Sales Outstanding (DSO)} - \text{Days Payable Outstanding (DPO)}$$

```
┌───────────────────────────────────────┬───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ Working Capital Lever                 │ Operational Mechanism                                         │
├───────────────────────────────────────┼───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ **DIO (Days Inventory: ~30-45 days)** │ Fast factory runs, regional C&F buffer stock, automated replenishment│
│ **DSO (Days Sales: ~5-15 days)**      │ Distributors pay via RTGS / cash advances before dispatch     │
│ **DPO (Days Payable: ~70-110 days)**  │ Massive scale purchasing power enables long supplier credit   │
│ **Net CCC: (-20 to -40 Days)**        │ **Company operates on vendor capital, generating massive cash**│
└───────────────────────────────────────┴───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

## 6. Essential KPI Formulas & Benchmark Ranges

### Distribution & Sales Performance Metrics

| Metric | Formula | Normal Benchmark Range |
|---|---|---|
| **Numeric Distribution (%)** | $$\text{Numeric Reach \%} = \frac{\text{Number of Stores Stocking the Brand SKU}}{\text{Total Universe of Stores Selling Category}} \times 100$$ | Mass Brand: **70% – 85%**<br>Niche/Premium: **20% – 35%** |
| **Weighted Distribution (ACV %)** | $$\text{Weighted Reach \%} = \frac{\text{Total Category Sales of Stores Stocking the Brand}}{\text{Total Category Sales Across Entire Market}} \times 100$$ | Healthy Brand: **Weighted > Numeric** (Stocked in top-selling stores) |
| **Trade Spend as % of Gross Sales** | $$\text{Trade Spend \%} = \frac{\text{Total Off-Invoice + Rebates + Slotting + Visibility Fees}}{\text{Gross Billed Revenue}} \times 100$$ | General Trade: **4% – 8%**<br>Modern Trade / Q-Comm: **15% – 25%** |
| **Same-Store Sales Growth (SSSG)** | $$\text{SSSG \%} = \frac{\text{Sales in Comparable Stores (Open >12 Mos) Yr } t - \text{Yr } t-1}{\text{Sales in Comparable Stores Yr } t-1} \times 100$$ | Mature FMCG: **6% – 12%** |
| **Share of Voice (SOV) vs SOM** | $$\text{SOV to SOM Ratio} = \frac{\text{Brand Advertising Spend / Category Ad Spend}}{\text{Brand Market Share / Total Category Market Share}}$$ | **>1.0x** required to expand market share (excess share of voice) |

#### Diagnostic Execution Protocol:
1. **Audit Channel Shift**: Did channel share swing from Kiranas (77% realization) to Quick Commerce/Modern Trade (54–58% realization)?
2. **Deconstruct Trade Promotions**: Are trade discounts being pocketed by wholesalers (wholesaler wholesale arbitrage) rather than passed to consumers?
3. **Execute SKU Tail Rationalization**: Map cumulative revenue vs SKUs. Typically, **top 20% of SKUs generate 80% of revenue and 110% of profits**, while bottom 40% lose money on short factory runs and expired depot inventory.

## 8. Common Pitfalls & Candidate "Landmines"

1. **The Primary Sales as Consumer Demand Illusion**:
   - *Candidate Error*: Looking only at factory dispatch figures (primary sales) to conclude that a brand is growing rapidly.
   - *MBB Reality*: Primary sales can easily be inflated by channel stuffing and aggressive distributor credit terms. True health must be validated via **secondary sales (distributor to retailer)** and **tertiary consumer offtake (Nielsen retail audits)**.

2. **Assuming Modern Trade and Quick Commerce are Higher Margin**:
   - *Candidate Error*: Advising an FMCG brand to aggressively exit General Trade (Kiranas) and focus exclusively on Modern Trade and Quick Commerce to "improve margins."
   - *MBB Reality*: Modern Trade and Quick Commerce demand **20%–30% margins + slotting fees + mandatory deep consumer discounts**, driving net realization down by 1500–2500 bps compared to General Trade.

3. **Treating Grammage Reduction (Shrinkflation) as Free Margin**:
   - *Candidate Error*: Recommending reducing a biscuit pack from 100g to 80g while keeping the price at INR 10 without evaluating competitor benchmarks.
   - *MBB Reality*: Consumers in price-sensitive emerging markets are highly perceptive to "perceived value in hand." If a competitor maintains grammage, the brand risks rapid market share loss in General Trade.

4. **Neglecting Numeric vs. Weighted Distribution**:
   - *Candidate Error*: Stating that a product has "great distribution" because it is in 500,000 stores.
   - *MBB Reality*: If those 500,000 stores are tiny rural kiosks that account for only 10% of category sales, the Numeric Reach is high (50%) but the **Weighted Reach is abysmal (10%)**, resulting in disappointing revenue.

5. **Unchecked SKU Proliferation**:
   - *Candidate Error*: Recommending launching 15 new flavor variants and package sizes to capture every consumer niche.
   - *MBB Reality*: Each new SKU dilutes factory changeover efficiency, fragments distributor safety stock, increases depot working capital, and confuses consumers at the shelf, often resulting in high inventory write-offs.
