# Heavy Manufacturing & Steel Industry Dossier

## 1. Industry Overview & Macro Dynamics

### 1.1 Core Business Model & Macro Drivers
The steel and heavy manufacturing sector converts bulk mineral commodities (iron ore, metallurgical coking coal, limestone, recycled steel scrap) into structural and engineered alloys used across construction, infrastructure, automotive, yellow goods/machinery, and energy transmission.

The industry is governed by five fundamental characteristics:
1. **Extreme Capital Intensity & Long Gestation:** Building an integrated greenfield 5 Million Tonnes Per Annum (MTPA) steel plant requires \$4B–\$6B in upfront Capex and 4–6 years of construction and commissioning.
2. **High Operating Leverage:** Fixed costs (depreciation, plant maintenance overhead, debt service, core engineering staff) account for 30–45% of total manufacturing cost. Plants must operate above **75–80% Capacity Utilization** to break even.
3. **Severe Commodity Cyclicality:** Steel is a global commodity priced off international benchmarks (e.g., FOB China / CFR East Asia HRC export prices). Spreads fluctuate wildly with global macroeconomic cycles, infrastructure spending, Chinese real estate activity, and currency shifts.
4. **Energy & Carbon Intensity:** Steel production accounts for ~7–9% of global greenhouse gas emissions. Traditional integrated Blast Furnace steelmaking emits 1.8–2.2 metric tonnes of $\text{CO}_2$ per tonne of crude steel.
5. **Geopolitical & Trade Protectionism:** Tariffs (Section 232 in the US), anti-dumping duties, safeguard quotas, and carbon border adjustment mechanisms (EU CBAM) heavily dictate export competitiveness.

### 1.2 Product Segmentation: Flats vs. Longs vs. Special Alloys
- **Flat Products (55–65% of global market):** Hot-Rolled Coils (HRC), Cold-Rolled Coils (CRC), Galvanized / Coated Sheets, Electrical Steel.
  - *End-Users:* Automotive OEMs, appliances, yellow goods, large-diameter oil & gas pipes.
  - *Characteristics:* High value-add, strict metallurgical tolerances, long-term 6–12 month OEM pricing contracts, higher EBITDA margins (\$100–\$180/tonne).
- **Long Products (30–40% of global market):** Rebars (TMT bars), Wire Rods, Beams, Angles, Heavy Structural Sections.
  - *End-Users:* Real estate construction, civil infrastructure, bridges, railways.
  - *Characteristics:* Highly commoditized, spot pricing, intense competition from secondary mini-mills, lower EBITDA margins (\$40–\$90/tonne).
- **Specialty & Alloy Steels (5–10%):** Stainless steel, tool steel, aerospace superalloys. Extreme margin, low volume.

### 2.1 The Two Primary Steelmaking Routes

| Parameter | Blast Furnace – Basic Oxygen Furnace (BF-BOF) | Electric Arc Furnace (EAF) / Induction Furnace (IF) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Primary Feedstock** | Iron Ore (Lump/Pellet) + Metallurgical Coking Coal | Recycled Steel Scrap and/or Direct Reduced Iron (DRI / Sponge Iron) |
| **Energy Source** | Chemical energy from Coal/Coke combustion | Electricity (Grid power / Renewable / Captive) |
| **Upfront Capex** | **High** (\$1,000–\$1,400 per annual tonne capacity) | **Moderate** (\$300–\$500 per annual tonne capacity) |
| **Minimum Economic Scale**| 3.0 to 5.0 MTPA (Mega-plant) | 0.5 to 1.5 MTPA (Modular Mini-mill) |
| **Variable Cost Share** | 55–65% (Cheaper bulk raw material) | 75–85% (High electricity + scrap cost) |
| **Operational Flexibility**| **Inflexible:** Blast furnaces must run 24/7 continuously; shutting down destroys refractory lining (\$10M+ relining cost). | **Highly Flexible:** Can stop/start within hours based on peak/off-peak power tariffs and scrap prices. |
| **$\text{CO}_2$ Emissions** | 1.8 – 2.2 tonnes $\text{CO}_2$ / tonne steel | 0.4 – 0.8 tonnes $\text{CO}_2$ / tonne steel (even lower with green power) |
| **Product Capability** | Full range: Ultra-clean high-spec auto sheet, electrical steel, heavy plates. | Historically longs/rebars; advancing into flat products with clean DRI charges. |

## 3. Revenue Models & Monetization Mechanics

### 3.1 Steel Pricing & Spread Mechanics
Steel manufacturers generate revenue through the **Steel Conversion Spread**:
$$\text{Conversion Spread (\$/tonne)} = \text{Finished Steel Price (HRC/Rebar)} - \text{Raw Material Basket Cost per Tonne Output}$$
- **The Raw Material Basket for 1 Tonne Liquid Steel (BF-BOF Route):**
  $$\text{Raw Material Cost} \approx (1.60 \times \text{Iron Ore Price}) + (0.75 \times \text{Coking Coal Price}) + (0.25 \times \text{Flux/Scrap/Alloys})$$
  - *Example:* If Iron Ore = \$110/t and Coking Coal = \$240/t:
    $$\text{Raw Material Cost} = (1.6 \times 110) + (0.75 \times 240) + 35 = 176 + 180 + 35 = \$391 / \text{tonne}$$
  - If HRC sells at \$620/tonne $\rightarrow$ Gross Spread = $\$620 - \$391 = \$229 / \text{tonne}$.
  - After $\$130/\text{tonne}$ conversion opex $\rightarrow$ EBITDA = $\$99 / \text{tonne}$ (~16% margin).

### 3.2 Sales Channels & Commercial Contracts
1. **Direct OEM Long-Term Contracts (35–50% of volume):**
   - 6-month or 12-month fixed-price or formula-linked contracts (indexed to Platts/Argus indices with 1-quarter lag).
   - Guarantees volume; protects plant utilization during downturns.
2. **Dealer / Stockist Spot Distribution (40–55% of volume):**
   - Dispatched to independent distributors and steel service centers on 15–30 day payment terms.
   - Prices adjust weekly based on domestic demand, inventory levels at ports, and import parity prices.
3. **Institutional & Project Tenders (10–15%):**
   - Government highway, bridge, and railway projects with formal EPC bidding.

## 6. Essential KPI Formulas & Benchmarks

### 6.1 Capacity Utilization Rate
$$\text{Capacity Utilization (\%)} = \frac{\text{Actual Finished Steel Produced (MT)}}{\text{Nominal Rated Annual Capacity (MT)}} \times 100$$
- **Benchmark:**
  - $>85\%$: High profitability, maximum fixed cost absorption.
  - $75–85\%$: Normal operating zone.
  - $<70\%$: Severe margin compression; fixed depreciation and debt interest wipe out net profit.

### 6.2 Yield Loss & Scrap Rate
$$\text{Yield (\%)} = \frac{\text{Saleable Finished Steel (MT)}}{\text{Liquid Crude Steel Input (MT)}} \times 100$$
$$\text{Yield Loss / Internal Scrap Rate (\%)} = 100\% - \text{Yield (\%)}$$
- **Benchmark:** Continuous casting + modern Hot Strip Mill achieves **94%–97% yield**. Poor secondary mills have 88%–91% yield (3–6% loss represents molten metal lost to scale, crop ends, and rejected coils).

### 6.3 Specific Energy Consumption (SEC)
$$\text{Specific Energy Consumption} = \frac{\text{Total Energy Consumed (Gcal or GJ)}}{\text{Tonne of Crude Steel Produced}}$$
- **Benchmark:**
  - Global Best Practice (Modern BF-BOF): 4.8 – 5.4 Gcal/tcs (Gigacalories per tonne crude steel).
  - Indian / Emerging Market Average: 5.5 – 6.3 Gcal/tcs.
  - Energy represents ~20–25% of total conversion cost; a 0.5 Gcal/tcs reduction saves millions annually.

### 6.4 Tap-to-Tap Time (EAF / BOF Cycle Time)
$$\text{Tap-to-Tap Time (mins)} = \text{Time from one batch molten tap to the next batch tap}$$
- Measures furnace throughput. Benchmark: Modern BOF: 35–45 minutes; Modern EAF: 40–50 minutes.

### 6.5 Working Capital Cash Conversion Cycle (CCC)
$$\text{CCC (Days)} = \text{Days Inventory Outstanding (DIO)} + \text{Days Sales Outstanding (DSO)} - \text{Days Payables Outstanding (DPO)}$$
- Steel requires massive working capital: Iron ore/coal stockyard inventory (45–60 days), work-in-progress slabs/billets (10–15 days), finished warehouse inventory (20–30 days). Target CCC: 45–65 days.

### 7.1 Case Scenario 1: Sourcing Consolidation & Quality Differentiation (IIMA Case 022 Model)
**Problem Statement:** An integrated steel manufacturer in central India producing sheets, rods, and pipes has industry-average profitability of 4.5% and seeks cost reduction opportunities. Sourcing costs and inventory levels are high.

**Diagnostic Protocol:**
1. **Value Chain Cost Breakdown:**
   - Sourcing accounts for 62% of total costs.
   - *Observation:* The client buys iron ore and fluxes from over 45 fragmented local suppliers.
   - *Opportunity:* **Supplier Consolidation:** Consolidate to top 5 Tier-1 suppliers in exchange for guaranteed annual purchase volume and 4–7% volume rebates.
2. **Manufacturing Process & Product Quality Alignment:**
   - *Observation:* The plant runs a single blast furnace producing ultra-high-quality crude steel for automotive sheets, rods, and industrial pipes alike.
   - *Insight:* Pipe customers do not require premium metallurgical purity.
   - *Action:* Introduce **internal scrap blending** (re-melting internal crop-ends and lower-cost sponge iron) on a dedicated secondary line for pipe manufacturing, lowering raw material cost by 12% on the pipe product line.
3. **Warehouse Inventory Pile-Up & Working Capital Unlock:**
   - *Observation:* Finished goods sit in storage for 45+ days because shipments are held when customers delay payment on prior orders.
   - *Action:* Transition from rigid 30-day credit to **2/10 Net 30 terms** (2% cash discount if paid within 10 days). Speeds up receivables collection by 20 days, slashes warehouse carrying costs, and frees up \$35M in operating cash flow.

## 8. Common Pitfalls & Candidate "Landmines"

1. **Assuming a Steel Plant Can Easily Be Shut Down During Demand Lulls:**
   - *Landmine:* Recommending a blast furnace plant halt operations for two months during a seasonal price dip.
   - *Reality:* Blast furnaces cannot simply be switched on and off. Shutting down a blast furnace (banking or blowing out) risks catastrophic refractory collapse, costs \$10M–\$30M to restart, and takes weeks. Integrated plants will continue producing at a cash loss as long as price covers short-run variable costs.

2. **Treating All Iron Ore and Coal as Uniform Commodities:**
   - *Landmine:* Assuming \$80/tonne iron ore is always better to buy than \$110/tonne ore.
   - *Reality:* Lower Fe grade ore (e.g., 58% Fe vs. 64% Fe) requires more limestone flux, consumes vastly more coking coal per tonne of hot metal, generates excessive slag, and reduces blast furnace daily output by 15–20%. The "Value-in-Use" (VIU) calculation often favors higher-priced, high-grade ore.

3. **Ignoring Captive Raw Material Integration:**
   - *Landmine:* Comparing EBITDA margins between Tata Steel and a non-integrated re-roller without adjusting for captive iron ore mines.
   - *Reality:* A steelmaker with 100% captive iron ore mines produces ore at \$18/tonne mining cost while merchant competitors buy at \$110/tonne market price—yielding a permanent \$150/tonne structural margin advantage.

4. **Underestimating Decarbonization Capex & CBAM Carbon Tax Exposure:**
   - *Landmine:* Modeling 2030 export projections to Europe without factoring in the EU Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism (CBAM).
   - *Reality:* A traditional BF-BOF plant exporting steel with 2.0 t $\text{CO}_2$/t steel into Europe will face carbon penalties exceeding \$150–\$200/tonne, rendering high-carbon exports obsolete unless transitioning to Green Hydrogen-DRI or purchasing certified carbon offsets.
